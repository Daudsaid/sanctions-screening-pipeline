# Evaluation

Run with `make eval` (after `make candidates score`). The tables between the
markers are regenerated by eval/evaluate.py; the text around them is written
by hand and has to be checked against new numbers. `--seed N --no-write`
reruns with another sample without touching this file.

## Method

True matches occur too rarely in a slice of UK fund managers to measure
recall, so the evaluation plants synthetic ones. It samples 200 person and
200 organisation sanctions targets, from the UK Sanctions List and the US
OFAC list merged, whose primary name is in Latin script, and makes one fake
subject per target with one realistic change:

- persons: a typo, surname first, a middle name dropped, a middle name
  added, or a transliteration variant (MOHAMMED / MUHAMMAD, SERGEY / SERGEI);
  40 of each
- organisations: a typo, two words swapped, a UK legal suffix added
  (LIMITED, LTD, PLC), or UK / GROUP added before one; 50 of each

A typo is one substitution, deletion, transposition or doubled letter in one
word of three or more letters. Organisation plants go through the same
legal-form stripping as int_name_norm, using the legal_forms seed in the
database.

Person plants get a Companies House style birth year and month. About half
agree with the target and are true matches. The rest are at least two years
from every target date and count as negatives. Person targets with no birth
date are left out of the sample. Plants have no nationality, so nationality
adds nothing either way.

The sample is stable across data refreshes. Targets are taken in an order
fixed by a hash of the seed and the target id, and each plant draws from its
own generator seeded the same way. Adding or removing a few targets changes
only those plants. (The first version shuffled the whole pool, so removing
187 delisted targets reshuffled every plant and moved recall by four points
with no change to matching.)

Plants are scored by the production code: matching.block.candidate_pairs
against every sanctions name of the same kind, then matching.score.score_pair
with the production word weights. A plant counts only against the target it
was made from. Every real Companies House subject is treated as a negative;
their scores come from match_score. The column "persons without DOB" rescores
the true person plants with no birth date, as for an officer Companies House
holds without one (3,103 secretaries in this slice).

### Spread across samples

The tables above are one sample (seed 20261014). The same run with four
other seeds shows how far a single sample can be trusted:

| At 70 | Range over 5 seeds |
| --- | --- |
| Recall, all true plants | 93-96% |
| Recall, persons with a matching birth date | 96-99% |
| Recall, persons on name alone | 87-98% |
| Recall, organisations | 91-94% |
| Recall, organisation typos | 72-92% |
| DOB-conflict plants alerting | 0 on every seed |
| Real pairs (not sampled) | 38 on every seed |

Overall recall is good to a couple of points. A single change type, with
16-50 plants, can move ten points or more between samples, so per-type
figures show where the weaknesses are, not how big they are.

## Recommended threshold: 70

Alert at a score of 70 or more. At 70, 93-96% of true plants alert across
five samples (95% in the tables above), including 96-99% of person plants
with a matching birth date. On the real data that is 38 subject-target
pairs (8 person, 30 organisation) across about 10,100 officers and 5,400
companies, which matching/alert.py groups into 30 alerts to review. No
DOB-conflict plant alerts at 65 or above, because a 40-point conflict caps a
perfect name at 60, or 65 with a nationality match; 70 keeps a margin above
that ceiling. Raising the threshold to 80 roughly halves the work (20 pairs,
18 alerts) but costs 1-3 points of overall recall and drops name-only person
recall to 74-88%. Lowering it to 65 adds 10 alerts for about a point of
recall. Each real alert is a few minutes of analyst time, while a missed
designated party is a breach, so 70 stands. Adding the UK list did not
change this choice.

## Where recall is low, and why

**Organisation typos (72-92% at 70 across samples).** Two words count as the
same when fuzz.ratio is at least 85. fuzz.ratio is based on insertions and
deletions, so one substituted or transposed letter costs two edits: a
six-letter word scores 83 (NEWTON / NWWTON) and only words of seven letters
or more survive a substitution. Words of four letters or fewer must match
exactly (QESHM IR / QESHM AIR, ROYL ARYA / ROYAL ARYA). The misspelt word is
then unmatched, and because it appears nowhere else it gets the highest
rarity weight, which pulls the score down hardest. Short organisation names
with one distinctive word (MELI MARINE / MELFI MARINE) suffer most.

**Person typos and transliteration in short names.** The same arithmetic
hits two-word person names: SALAH ADI / SALAH BADI scores 56 and MOHAMMAD
SAID / MOHAMED SAID 68 even with a matching birth date. Longer names and
the birth-date bonus carry most person plants over the line, so this shows
up as a few misses per sample rather than a low rate.

**UK / GROUP added to a one-word name (98% up to 80).** One plant per sample
never becomes a candidate: PLANAR GROUP against the one-word target PLANAR
in this run (TRIALKOM in an earlier one). token_sort_ratio is below 80, and
the token_set rule requires both names to have two words, a guard that stops
one-word targets matching every name that contains them. A UK subsidiary of
a single-word designated company is exactly this case, so it is a real gap
in candidate generation.

The weights were not changed in response to these results. Likely fixes for
a later version are a word-level edit distance that counts a substitution as
one edit (for example rapidfuzz's Levenshtein normalised similarity), and
letting token_set_ratio through when the one-word side is rare. Both should
be measured against this evaluation, over several seeds, and against the
alert volume on real subjects.

## Limits of this evaluation

- Plants have exactly one change. Real matches can combine several (a
  transliteration and a dropped middle name), so recall here is an upper
  bound on single-change cases.
- Plants are built from primary names. Aliases are matched too, which helps
  real matches but isn't measured separately.
- Real subjects are assumed to be negatives. The pairs at 70 or above were
  reviewed by name, birth date and nationality (see docs/decisions.md,
  "Alerts" and "UK Sanctions List"). None is an obvious true match, but a
  few need an analyst with more than this data: [company]
  against [sanctioned firm] (listed with a UK link), [officer]
  (nationality matches, birth date five months off) and [officer] (birth
  date one month off, nationality differs).
- The real-alert counts are for this slice of about 15,500 subjects. They
  will scale with the register, and organisation alerts are driven by
  generic words that a larger slice will contain more of.
