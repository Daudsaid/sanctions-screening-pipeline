# Evaluation

Run with `make eval` (after `make candidates score`). The tables between the
markers are regenerated by eval/evaluate.py; the text around them is written
by hand and has to be checked against new numbers.

## Method

True matches occur too rarely in a slice of UK fund managers to measure
recall, so the evaluation plants synthetic ones. With a fixed seed it samples
200 OFAC person targets and 200 organisation targets whose primary name is in
Latin script, and makes one fake subject per target with one realistic
change:

- persons: a typo, surname first, a middle name dropped, a middle name
  added, or a transliteration variant (MOHAMMED / MUHAMMAD, SERGEY / SERGEI);
  40 of each
- organisations: a typo, two words swapped, a UK legal suffix added
  (LIMITED, LTD, PLC), or UK / GROUP added before one; 50 of each

A typo is one substitution, deletion, transposition or doubled letter in one
word of three or more letters. Organisation plants go through the same
legal-form stripping as int_name_norm, using the legal_forms seed in the
database.

Person plants get a Companies House style birth year and month. Half agree
with the target and are true matches. The other half are at least two years
from every target date and count as negatives. Person targets with no birth
date (1.3%) are left out of the sample. Plants have no nationality, so
nationality adds nothing either way.

Plants are scored by the production code: matching.block.candidate_pairs
against every OFAC name of the same kind, then matching.score.score_pair with
the production word weights. A plant counts only against the target it was
made from. Every real Companies House subject is treated as a negative; their
scores come from match_score. The column "persons without DOB" rescores the
true person plants with no birth date, as for an officer Companies House
holds without one (3,103 secretaries in this slice).

## Recommended threshold: 70

Alert at a score of 70 or more. At 70, 97% of true plants alert (291 of 300),
including every person plant with a matching birth date and 96% of person
plants scored on name alone. On the real data that is 33 subject-target
pairs (5 person, 28 organisation) across about 10,100 officers and 5,400
companies, which matching/alert.py groups into 26 alerts to review: one
company alone accounts for six pairs. No
DOB-conflict plant alerts at 65 or above, because a 40-point conflict caps a
perfect name at 60, or 65 with a nationality match. 70 keeps a margin above
that ceiling. Raising the threshold to 80 halves the real pairs (16) but
loses six true plants, mostly organisation typos, and name-only person recall
falls from 96% to 86%. Each real alert is a few minutes of analyst time,
while a missed designated party is a breach, so the extra 12 alerts (17
pairs) are worth it. Going below 65 buys almost no recall and lets birth-date conflicts
through. The sample is small (20 true plants per person change type), so
these figures are estimates to within a few points.

## Where recall is low, and why

**Organisation typos (84% at 70, 74% at 80).** Two words count as the same
when fuzz.ratio is at least 85. fuzz.ratio is based on insertions and
deletions, so one substituted or transposed letter costs two edits: a
six-letter word scores 83 (NEWTON / NWWTON) and only words of seven letters
or more survive a substitution. Words of four letters or fewer must match
exactly (KSK / KKSK, BANK / JANK). The misspelt word is then unmatched, and
because it appears nowhere else it gets the highest rarity weight, which
pulls the score down hardest. Short organisation names with one distinctive
word (NEWTON TRADING, MEUSE SHIPPING) suffer most. Person typos do better
because their names have more words, and the birth-date bonus lifts them.

**Transliteration (95% at 80, 75% at 90).** The same arithmetic applies:
SERGEY / SERGEI is one substitution in six letters, so the words don't match
and the plant relies on the surname and birth date. Short variant pairs such
as OMAR / UMAR and YURI / YURIY fail the exact-match rule for short words.

**UK / GROUP added (98% up to 80, 64% at 90).** One plant never becomes a
candidate: TRIALKOM GROUP against the one-word target TRIALKOM.
token_sort_ratio is 73, and the token_set rule requires both names to have
two words, a guard that stops one-word targets matching every name that
contains them. A UK subsidiary of a single-word designated company is
exactly this case, so it is a real gap in candidate generation. The others
lose a few points for the extra word, which matters only above 85.

The weights were not changed in response to these results. Likely fixes for
a later version are a word-level edit distance that counts a substitution as
one edit (for example rapidfuzz's Levenshtein normalised similarity), and
letting token_set_ratio through when the one-word side is rare. Both should
be measured against this evaluation and against the alert volume on real
subjects.

## Limits of this evaluation

- Plants have exactly one change. Real matches can combine several (a
  transliteration and a dropped middle name), so recall here is an upper
  bound on single-change cases.
- Plants are built from primary names. Aliases are matched too, which helps
  real matches but isn't measured separately.
- Real subjects are assumed to be negatives. All 33 pairs at 70 or above
  were reviewed by name, birth date and nationality (see docs/decisions.md,
  "Alerts"). None is an obvious true match, but a few need an analyst with
  more than this data: [company] against [sanctioned firm] (listed with a UK link), [officer] (nationality matches,
  birth date five months off) and [officer] (birth date one month off,
  nationality differs).
- The real-alert counts are for this slice of about 15,500 subjects. They
  will scale with the register, and organisation alerts are driven by
  generic words that a larger slice will contain more of.
